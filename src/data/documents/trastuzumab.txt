Trastuzumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody used in the treatment of HER2-positive breast cancer. It works by binding to the extracellular domain of the HER2 receptor, a protein encoded by the ERBB2 gene that is overexpressed in roughly 15-20% of breast cancers. By blocking HER2 signaling, trastuzumab slows the proliferation of cancer cells and also flags them for destruction by the immune system. Trastuzumab is typically administered alongside chemotherapy in early-stage disease and has significantly improved survival outcomes for patients with HER2-amplified tumors. It is often combined with pertuzumab, another HER2-targeted antibody, to more completely block receptor dimerization.
